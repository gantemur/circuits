# Reading the circuit kernel

The maintained TypeScript in `src/` is meant to be read alongside [the sign and unit conventions](MATHEMATICAL_CONVENTIONS.md). A device's ordered terminals are `(p,n)`: voltage is `V(p)−V(n)`, current is positive from `p` to `n`, and absorbed power is their product. Ground `0` is the voltage reference. The kernel is independent of the browser UI, and generated `dist/` and `publish/` bundles are outputs rather than teaching source.

## From a document to a checked answer

1. `model.ts` defines the strict native `{circuit,experiment}` document. `parseDocument` and `validateDocument` reject unsupported fields, missing terminals, incomplete source values, and invalid initial conditions. `sourceAt` evaluates a time waveform with an explicit left or right side at a step.
2. `assembly.ts` implements device laws in `assemble`. `incidence` makes each branch's `+1` at `p` and `−1` at `n` (ground's voltage coordinate is omitted). Unknowns are non-ground node voltages followed by selected branch currents. Its contribution records identify each entry of `E`, `K`, or `B` in `E ẋ + K x = B u`; `currentRow` turns the same laws into measured currents.
3. `complex.ts` holds rectangular complex arithmetic. `numeric.ts` solves the small dense real or complex systems by row/column equilibration and complete-pivot Gaussian elimination. `solveLinear` reports rank, a scaled backward residual, and a pivot ratio; neither is a certified condition number. `residuals` checks the *unscaled* equations in amperes for KCL and volts for device laws.
4. `analysis.ts` selects DC or RMS AC source coordinates and solves `(K+sE)x=Bu` in `solveDC`, `solveFrequency`, and `transfer`. `describeSolution` evaluates observations, checks physical residuals, and reconstructs ground KCL. `extractFirstOrder` performs only its declared one-storage-coordinate elimination, returning an explicit unsupported outcome otherwise.
5. `transient.ts` solves the storage-constrained initialization in `initialize`, then `transient` advances by backward Euler, `(K+E/h)x_{n+1}=Bu_{n+1}+Ex_n/h`. It lands on scheduled source steps and reinitializes their right side without silently breaking capacitor-voltage or inductor-current continuity.
6. `one-port.ts` runs separate DC open, suppressed-source test, short, and loaded solves in `dcOnePort`. `kron.ts` builds the full ungrounded resistor Laplacian and computes its terminal Schur complement by solves. `resistor-transform.ts` applies the six supported source-free positive-resistor moves, then `checkResponse` independently checks ordered response matrices, internal KCL, terminal currents, and physical power. Terminal equivalence does not claim equal internal states.
7. `port-analysis.ts` builds temporary current and voltage experiments on a strict native document. `analyzePorts` suppresses independent source values, optionally removes one explicitly external voltage drive, and retains controlled laws. `impedance` injects current from `q` to `p` to build each Z column; `admittance` clamps each port voltage and negates clamp-branch current to build Y independently. `predict` solves the two loading boundary equations for each available matrix; `directLoad` connects a physical 1 V RMS source, series resistor, and output load to the same network. `comparison` uses separate V and A limits. Per-stage `Solution` values retain the MNA solver's physical residual checks, but a full-state failure does not prove that a terminal description is mathematically absent.

## Three reading paths

**Voltage divider.** Open `fixtures/divider.json`, then follow its ordered resistor and voltage-source contributions through `assemble` and `currentRow`. With `E=0`, `solveDC` calls the linear solver and `describeSolution`. The independent divider, source-current, and power expectations are in `test/acceptance.test.mjs` (“A: divider…”). `test/one-port.test.mjs` separately derives its Thévenin/Norton terminal experiment.

**RC transient.** Start with `fixtures/rc.json`. In `assemble`, the capacitor contributes `C aaᵀ` to `E` and records its voltage `aᵀx` as a storage value. Read `initialize` before the stepping loop in `transient`; its augmented equations preserve algebraic constraints instead of inverting singular `E`. The backward Euler recurrence and refinement expectations are independently derived in `test/acceptance.test.mjs` (“E: RC step…”); event continuity cases follow nearby.

**Resistor reduction.** Start with `fixtures/res-star.json` or `fixtures/res-bridge.json`. `resistorLaplacian` stamps each conductance into the *full* Laplacian, including node `0`; `kronReduce` partitions retained and hidden indices. In `transformResistors`, inspect candidate validation, the selected operation, strict native preview generation, and `checkResponse`. `test/resistor-transform.test.mjs` derives star, inverse delta, weighted-current, and bridge expectations independently; `test/kron.test.mjs` covers range and nonfinite failures. The response and current checks are floating-point diagnostics, not certified error enclosures.

**Frequency-domain port and loading.** Start with `fixtures/port-rc-section.json` and follow `analyzePorts` into `characterizedNetwork`, then one `impedance` and `admittance` column. The temporary current source points `q→p`, so positive injected current enters the original network at `p`; an ideal voltage clamp has the opposite branch-current sign. Compare the independently solved Y entries with `[[1/R,−1/R],[−1/R,1/R+sC]]` and the positive-frequency Z entries with the formula in `test/port-analysis.test.mjs`. At DC the same test expects Y to succeed while Z fails. For loading, read `predict`'s two complex boundary equations and the separate direct circuit built in `directLoad`, then the resistor-T and connected-RC acceptance tests. Each matrix is 1×1 or 2×2, in Ω or S, and voltage/current discrepancies retain their own units. Matrix sampling and loaded agreement at one frequency are numerical evidence, not a proof about all frequencies or hidden state.

## Reading the Phase 3A1 diode kernel

Open `fixtures/nonlinear/resistor-diode.json` and read `src/nonlinear-model.ts` first. Its strict v2 parser validates the explicit diode law and uses a v1-only proxy to reuse existing node, source, observation, and controlled-source reference rules; it never passes that proxy to analysis. `src/diode-operating-point.ts` then calls `assembleDiodeSystem`: ordinary linear devices keep their `assemble` stamps, and each diode adds one p→n current coordinate. In `evaluateDiodeSystem`, follow the KCL incidence, the ampere-valued `iD−Is·expm1(vj/eta)` row, and its three analytical derivatives. The row labels distinguish A from V; no diode charge enters `E`.

Read `solveDiodeOperatingPoint` next. It solves the real Jacobian correction with `solveReal`, halves a trial step until the fixed-scale dimensionless residual merit decreases, and accepts a point only after the original nonlinear equations pass unit-aware physical row checks. `defaultCurrentScale` derives a fixed search weight from declared currents and conductances. `physicalContributions` groups device laws by branch voltage/current; `physicalRows` separates that model allowance from expanded matrix roundoff. `describe` reconstructs ground KCL, signed device power, and `v·i = vj·i + Rs·i²`. A failed correction or exhausted search is a numerical diagnosis, not a theorem about the root's existence or uniqueness. The separate `src/diode-tangent.ts` checks the solved circuit/DC-source bias fingerprint, substitutes each diode's local `rd = Rs+1/gj` as an ordinary native v1 resistor, zeroes DC and storage increments, and preserves the current document's declared AC increments. Its v1 circuit can be passed to `assemble` and `transfer`; a unit transfer is a local derivative.

The independent reading checks are in `test/phase-3a-diode.test.mjs`: two stated thermal-voltage benchmarks, scalar load-line bisection, a constructed series-resistance point, antiparallel signs, finite-difference Jacobian entries, neighboring-bias sensitivity refinement, and the external-capacitor frequency equation. Run `npm run demo:diode` for a labeled trace. The v1 parser remains strict to v1; the Phase 3A2 browser routes v1 and v2 before invoking either parser.

## Reading the Phase 3A3 transient kernel

Open `fixtures/nonlinear/diode-rc-step.json`, then `nonlinearTransient` in `src/diode-transient.ts`. It validates and bounds the augmented dimension before dense assembly, obtains the unchanged Phase 3A1 static residual/Jacobian through `assembleDiodeSystem` and `evaluateDiodeSystem`, and takes each capacitor/inductor `S` and `F` from linear `assemble`. `evaluate` constructs the compatible initializer `[r+Φw; Sx−q]` or backward-Euler stage `r+Φ(Sx−qprevious)/h`, with complete differential capacitor rows. `meritScales` gives coupled KCL and diode-law rows a shared A scale from actual source, resistor, controlled-current, and storage-companion values, fixed for each solve; `solve` then uses the real dense solver and backtracking. `checkedRows` independently enforces the original row-unit residual allowances. `sample` reconstructs every signed branch current, ground KCL, power and BE energy identity. Follow the separate current/voltage arithmetic estimates into ground KCL, W-valued power and J-valued energy allowances; these are diagnostic roundoff budgets, not altered physical tolerances. The grid guard snaps only a representably advanced nominal endpoint within a tightly capped binary64 rounding distance to the exact event/stop boundary *before* solving. Failed frozen equilibrium carries its original Newton corrections with empty storage-rate entries. The orchestration loop lands on source events, solves distinct left and right sides, and retains only accepted samples. The scalar diode–RC, rectifier, mixed storage, antiparallel, RK4 refinement, and failure acceptance cases in `test/phase-3a3-transient.test.mjs` are independent of production Newton. Run `npm run demo:diode-transient` for a concise labeled trace. The reviewed kernel itself does not depend on the Phase 3A4 browser adapter.

## Reading the Phase 3A4 browser transient path

Open the browser copy of `fixtures/nonlinear/diode-rc-step.json` in `web/presets.ts`. Its distinct component IDs keep the step and biased-sinusoid lessons separate even though their electrical layouts are similar. `web/workspace.ts` supplies suggested Transient panels and validates accepted duration, step, and named output without invoking the nonlinear solver on render. Follow the Run handler in `web/main.ts` into `TransientRunLifecycle.start` in `web/transient-run.ts`: the preflight counts N MNA/diode unknowns plus m storage-rate coordinates, bounds event-restarted intervals, snapshots the strict document and settings, and launches at most one worker. `scripts/build-app.mjs` embeds bundled maintained `web/nonlinear-worker.ts` in `app.js`, so the static package remains three files. The worker calls the unchanged `nonlinearTransient` once. `TransientRunLifecycle.reconcile` terminates and permanently retires an obsolete run; a late callback cannot restore it. Language and panel order do not change scientific identity.

Read `web/transient-presentation.ts` next. It plots only accepted observation samples and optional actual voltage-source values on a V axis, preserving consecutive same-time left/right values. Its selected-sample tables show exact IDs, A/V row residuals, W/J power and energy allowances, and correction units, without replacing the cached DC Q point. `transientCsv` includes accepted samples and a valueless failure marker, retaining exact IDs through CSV quoting. The independent scalar and event checks remain in `test/phase-3a3-transient.test.mjs`; `test/phase-3a4-browser.test.mjs` checks the worker contract, lifecycle, presentation and export path. An actual browser Run/Cancel check remains necessary to establish responsive Worker behavior beyond a Node harness. This work is on a review branch, not yet the released site.

## Reading the Phase 3A2 browser diode path

Start with `web/native-document.ts`: `parseBrowserDocument` dispatches on the required version and preserves the strict validators. `web/lab.ts` keeps the applied document and revision; `web/builder.ts` promotes a draft to v2 when D is added and uses the same parser at Apply. `web/presets.ts` provides four browser copies of independently checked v2 fixtures; `web/schematic.ts` draws them only after exact topology matching. Other topologies use the exact table.

For analysis, follow `PanelCalculator.nonlinear` in `web/workspace.ts`. One cached DC bias feeds DC, the selected diode's characteristic Q marker, and a provenance-checked tangent for frequency and equations. The selected source's declared RMS AC increments remain separate from the DC bias. `web/diode-presentation.ts` keeps a DC panel's chosen diode identity, lists its current augmented residual/Jacobian terms, and separates original Q/source values from zero-DC incremental excitation. `web/schematic.ts` labels the tangent's source and observation increments. `web/diode-math.ts` evaluates the terminal I–V relation; its sign-preserving junction-voltage bracket and reconstruction check keep `Rs` inside the device and expose unresolvable samples. `eligibleLoadLine` proves the external three-device series loop before drawing `I=(E−V)/R`. `web/dc-sweep.ts` copies the document at each sampled source value, yields between points, and seeds only from successful solutions. `web/sweep-lifecycle.ts` permanently retires a run when a production panel/workspace/document/visibility transition invalidates it, so restoring old controls cannot resurrect CSV. `web/plot.ts` normalizes finite ranges before coordinate subtraction and reports an explicit display-range failure if geometry remains unrepresentable. UI traces and panel settings never enter native JSON. `test/phase-3a2-browser.test.mjs` checks independent scalar answers, production transitions, sign and coordinate cases, and the same presentation helpers used in the browser.

## Fork and modify

Fork the repository, install Node 22 or newer, and run `npm ci`. Make changes on a branch. For a device law, state its terminal voltage/current orientation and expected contributions before editing `assemble`; for an analysis, state its experiment and units before changing a solve. Add an independent analytic fixture or orientation/residual test when behavior changes. Run `npm test`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, `npm run build:site`, and `git diff --check`. The browser app is built from the same kernel. Keep native JSON, public APIs, sign conventions, and failure classifications stable unless a separately reviewed task authorizes a change.

## Reading a visual resistor transformation

Start at `web/resistor-transform-ui.ts` with the ordered terminal selectors and `resistorCandidates` output. Candidate labels explain topology but do not certify a numerical result. Pressing Preview calls the unchanged kernel `transformResistors`, which validates the native circuit, performs the selected move, builds a strict transformed document, and runs independent matrix, internal-KCL, boundary-current, and power checks. The UI retains the document identity, revision, candidate key, and ordered terminals with that result. Any change invalidates application; Use re-runs the helper and compares the strict serialized output before importing it through the normal validated-document path. Return uses a session snapshot with the same editor-draft protections. `test/visual-learning.test.mjs` exercises the explicit boundary and use/return; `test/resistor-transform.test.mjs` contains the independent physical identities.

For the displayed boundary experiment, `terminalExperiment` multiplies each finite row of the ordered N×N response Λ (S) by a declared N-vector V (V). One non-ground terminal is at 1 V and all other terminals, including native `0`, are clamped to 0 V. The resulting N currents (A) enter each network. This single illustration does not verify equivalence; the helper's independent multi-vector checks do that. Nonfinite display arithmetic produces no current claim. `web/network-diagram.ts` draws the exact before/after resistor graph from the helper's networks, with retained terminals at corresponding positions; `web/schematic.ts` uses curated positions only after `matchingPreset` checks the actual topology. Guide lessons and independently authored baseline response metadata live in `web/study-guide.ts` and `web/reference-responses.ts`, outside the native scientific schema. Foster/Cauer synthesis concerns admissible driving-point functions; a voltage-transfer target is a different realization problem. This milestone implements neither synthesis path.

## Reading the Phase 2D2 examples

For a connected filter, open `fixtures/rc3-low-pass.json` and trace each R/C terminal through `assemble`, then its `Vin → out` numerical transfer through `transfer`. `test/phase-2d2.test.mjs` independently evaluates the three-node equations summarized in [the Phase 2D2 specification](PHASE_2D2_SPECIFICATION.md), including the load experiment, event continuity, and first-order time-step refinement. The Butterworth fixtures use the existing VCVS law; follow the control terms in `assembly.ts` and verify that `Cf` or `Rf` really returns from `out` to `a`. The RLC pair illustrates that a changed observation can change the named response without changing the series R/L/C laws. These examples do not broaden `extractFirstOrder`.

For a source-free driving-point example, open `fixtures/rc-impedance-ladder.json` or `fixtures/rl-impedance-ladder.json`. `analyzePorts` makes temporary current-injection and voltage-clamp experiments on copies; the native fixture remains source-free. Read the tail-first series/shunt continued fraction in the fixture catalogue, then compare the independent complex values and fixed SI-s coefficients in `test/phase-2d2.test.mjs`. The RC ladder's DC Y succeeds while the current-driven full-state Z test cannot return a finite matrix. `web/presets.ts` registers fixtures without adding UI fields to native JSON; `web/workspace.ts` sets fresh panel suggestions; `web/schematic.ts` draws verified endpoint layouts; `web/phase-2d2-guides.ts` and `web/reference-responses.ts` provide bilingual lessons and authored baseline targets. Changing an element affects kernel results, not those authored targets.
